1. Executive Summary

UpliftX is a machine-learning application that identifies which customers a marketing campaign should actually target. Rather than predicting who is likely to convert, it predicts who converts because they were targeted — the customers a campaign genuinely influences. This lets a business avoid spending on people who would have converted anyway (or never will), and instead concentrate budget on the segment where outreach creates real incremental revenue.

The project is delivered as a five-page interactive Streamlit dashboard, backed by a modular Python pipeline that handles data ingestion, feature engineering, uplift modeling (a T-Learner built on two XGBoost classifiers), churn risk scoring, model evaluation (Qini curves and a Qini coefficient), and a business ROI simulator with adjustable cost/revenue assumptions.

2. Objective & Business Value

  • Objective: quantify the incremental effect of a marketing email on each customer, and use that to recommend an optimal targeting depth.
  • Business value: demonstrates how uplift modeling turns a marketing spend decision ("who do we email?") into a profit-optimization problem with a defensible, data-driven answer.
  • Audience: useful both as a working analytics tool and as a portfolio piece demonstrating causal/uplift ML applied end-to-end, from raw data to a business decision.

3. Dataset

The Hillstrom MineThatData E-Mail Analytics Challenge dataset — a real, randomized marketing experiment covering ~64,000 customers, each randomly assigned to a Men's email campaign, a Women's email campaign, or no email (control), with website visit outcomes tracked afterward. Randomization makes this one of the few public datasets where uplift models can be validated against ground truth without confounding.

4. Methodology

T-Learner Uplift Model

Model_T: XGBoost classifier fit on treated customers  → P(visit=1 | X, treated)
Model_C: XGBoost classifier fit on control customers  → P(visit=1 | X, control)

Uplift Score = Model_T(X) − Model_C(X)

Customers are ranked by this score, and the top-ranked customers are the "persuadables" — the highest-value targets for the campaign.

Evaluation

Model quality is assessed with a Qini curve (cumulative incremental conversions captured vs. random targeting), a single-number Qini coefficient (area between the model curve and the random baseline), and decile-level incremental conversion rates. Feature importances are extracted separately for Model_T and Model_C to understand what drives response under each condition.

Churn Risk

A separate XGBoost classifier flags customers unlikely to convert regardless of treatment — useful for suppression lists, distinct from the uplift-driven targeting strategy.

Business Simulation

Given a cost-per-treatment and revenue-per-conversion assumption, the app evaluates ten candidate targeting depths (top 10%–100% of customers by uplift score) and reports the profit-maximizing depth, with full cost/revenue/ROI breakdown and CSV export.

5. System Architecture

Hillstrom CSV → load & preprocess → feature engineering (scale + one-hot encode) │ ├──▶ T-Learner (Model_T, Model_C) → uplift scores → Qini evaluation │ → business ROI simulation └──▶ Churn model → churn risk scores All stages are orchestrated by app/app.py (Streamlit) and cached so the dashboard is responsive after the first (one-time) training pass.

8. Limitations & Next Steps

  • Models retrain on every cold start rather than loading saved artifacts.
  • Evaluation uses a single train/test split — no cross-validation or confidence intervals yet.
  • Treatment is modeled as binary (any email vs. none); Men's/Women's campaigns aren't distinguished.
  • No automated test coverage on the evaluation/simulation math yet.
Recommended next steps: load pre-trained models on startup, add pytest coverage for evaluator.py/business_sim.py, and add S-Learner/X-Learner alternatives with a model-comparison view. Full roadmap in FUTURE_SUGGESTIONS.md.

9. Conclusion

UpliftX demonstrates a complete, working uplift-modeling pipeline — from a raw randomized-experiment dataset to a business-facing profit recommendation — wrapped in an interactive dashboard. It is functional, evaluable (via Qini metrics), and extensible, with a documented roadmap for further depth (additional meta-learners, cross-validation, automated testing, and pre-trained model loading).
